# History of Memetics

The concept of the meme has a much older and more controversial history than most people realize.

## The Origin Debate

### 1890–1904: Early precursors

- **Gabriel Tarde** (1890): French sociologist who wrote "Les lois de l'imitation" — focused on imitation and cultural diffusion
- **Richard Semon** (1904): Evolutionary biologist who wrote "Die Mneme" — defined memes as "units for cultural transmission of experiences"

### 1920s–1930s

- **Maurice Maeterlinck** (1926): Belgian Nobel Prize winner wrote "The Life of the White Ant", mentioning the "mneme" as a memory trace
- Later accused of plagiarism from South African poet Eugène Marais

## The Dawkins Breakthrough

In "The Selfish Gene" (1976), Richard Dawkins coined the term **meme** (shortened from "mimeme", derived from Greek *mimeisthai* = to imitate). He acknowledged:
- **Ted Cloak** (1975) had outlined a similar hypothesis
- Work by **Cavalli-Sforza** (1973) and **Cullen** (1972)

## The 1980s: Culture Genes

Two controversial books laid groundwork:

- **Lumsden & Wilson** (1981): "Genes, mind and culture" — introduced "culture genes"
- **Cavalli-Sforza & Feldmann** (1981): "Cultural transitions and evolution"

Both accused of "orthodox biology" with a "nature over culture" agenda.

## The Sociology Wars

The term **sociobiology** became derogatory in many social science circles. Critics argued it:
- Had sole genetic determinism on culture
- Undermined perspectives on human freedom and choice
- Was influenced by **David Sahlins'** earlier book "The Use and Abuse of Biology" (1976)

## Alternative Paths

**Boyd & Richerson** (1985): "Culture and the evolution of culture" — offered an evolutionary understanding of culture without the memetic label, avoiding the heated sociology debates while contributing to the same field.

## The Original Muse

> *"Mneme was one of the three original Muses in Boeotia, ancient Greece. Her sisters were Aoide, the muse of song, and Melete, the muse of meditation. Mneme was the muse of memory."*
