# The Memetics Debate

At the heart of Memetics lies a fundamental philosophical split: **where do memes live?**

## The Three Questions

Memetics rests on three levels of inquiry:

1. **Ontological**: *Reality is memes.* Memes are the fundamental units of cultural reality.
2. **Epistemological**: *How does Memetics make sense of memes?* What can we know about them?
3. **Methodological**: *What investigatory techniques can be applied?* How do we study them?

But the ontological question is where the field split.

## The Great Divide: Externalists vs Internalists

### Externalists

Memes are **out there** — in the world, in artifacts, in behavior.

- **Gatherer** (1999): Memes are observable artifacts and behavior (signs). Outside the occurrence of the event, the practice of behavior or life of the artifact, memes have no existence.
- **Benzon** (1996): Memes exist in the physical "pool" of the environment — like tools, texts, songs, rituals.

> *"A meme only exists when it is manifested. No manifestation = no meme."*

**Think:** A recipe written in a cookbook exists whether or not anyone reads it. Is the recipe a meme?

### Internalists

Memes are **in here** — in the cognitive brain, as information patterns.

- **Heylighen** (1998): A meme is an information pattern held in an individual's memory, capable of being copied to another individual's memory. It's a memory item — a portion of an organism's neurally-stored information.
- **Lynch** (1998): A meme is identified using the abstraction system of the observer, whose instantiation depended critically on causation by prior instantiation of the same memory item in one or more organism's nervous system.

> *"A meme is a memory item. No brain = no meme."*

**Think:** The recipe only exists when someone has read it and remembers it. In the book it's just ink.

## Can Both Be Right?

The split isn't just academic. It changes how you study culture:

- **If memes are external** → study artifacts, texts, behaviors, patterns of social action
- **If memes are internal** → study memory, cognition, neural patterns, psychology

The field tried to be both at once — and that may have been its undoing.

## Aunger's Challenge

**Robert Aunger** (2002) put forward a radical skepticism: *no one actually knows what a meme is.*

Not a definition — a consensus. Not a theory — a shared agreement on the subject. The field had no stable object of study.

## The Criticism

In academic circles, Memetics came to be regarded as:

1. **An obscure philosophy** rather than a science
2. **A weak metaphor of genetics** — a powerful analogy that breaks down
3. **An over-ambitious ontology** — "Reality is memes" was too grand for the evidence

The metaphor of genes → memes works until you try to operationalize it. Genetics has:
- Measurable units (DNA sequences)
- Clear replication mechanisms (cell division)
- Observable mutation and selection

Memetics had none of these.

## The Stakes

If externalists are right, Memetics is a science of **culture as an ecology** — memes out there in the world, competing for attention, changing through contact.

If internalists are right, Memetics is a science of **the mind as a network** — memes as memory structures, copied, mutated, and selected within individual brains.

Both are compelling. Neither was ever tested decisively.
