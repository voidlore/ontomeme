# Ontological Memetics

> *"Memetics is the study of how memes spread in social systems and how they impact us as individual agents."*
> — Øyvind Vada

## What is a meme?

A **meme** is a sustainable unit of information that spreads through human culture. The concept was introduced by Richard Dawkins ~30 years ago.

Memes are both:
- **Semiotic signs** — things out there in the world (a word, a song, a ritual)
- **Objects in our minds** — the mental representation that gets copied

Examples: "Chair", "Jesus", "The smell of roses", "Beethoven no. 5".

## How memes spread

Memes don't spread by themselves — they have no consciousness or intent. From the outside, we can describe their movement using three evolutionary mechanisms:

1. **Diffusion** — memes move between people and cultures
2. **Selection** — humans actively adopt or reject memes
3. **Adaptation** — memes change to better fit the cultures they inhabit

Think of it like biological evolution, but for *information* instead of genes. The "competition" isn't between the memes themselves — it's between the cultural systems that host them.

## Memes and consciousness

Each person hosts thousands of memes simultaneously. The memes we carry depend on:
- The cultural systems we belong to
- The cultural systems we relate to

This is why the same word or idea can mean different things in different cultures — the surrounding meme-complex shapes its meaning.

## Key terms

- **Meme**: a sustainable information unit that influences and forms us
- **Diffusion**: the spread of memes among social systems
- **Selection**: memes are naturally selected by humans based on "competition" within consciousness
- **Adaptation**: memes change to fit the cultural systems they compete within
- **Meme complex**: a set of related memes that reinforce each other
