# Strengthening Memetics

Behind all the criticisms lies a constructive impulse: **how do we make Memetics work?**

## The Three Proposals

Several researchers agreed on what needs to happen:

### 1. Define a clear, distinctive domain ontology

Each memetic research project needs **mutually-exclusive memes** as units of analysis. Before you start, decide exactly what a meme is in your study — and stick to it.

### 2. Develop a sustainable memetic epistemology

Build a **scientific attractor** — a unifying framework that different research threads can revolve around, even when they disagree on specific theories or methods.

### 3. Define a clear methodological framework

Establish **distinct criteria for validity and reliability**. The epistemology should:
- Make sense of the diffusion and adaptation of predefined memes
- Explain their influence on individual and collective states and actions
- Require researchers to define meme units every time they enter a new empirical field

## Reuniting the Divide

The externalist/internalist split was counter-productive. Aunger proposed an **integrative definition**:

> Memes can be seen as both **signs and objects** AND as **forms and cognitive templates** that process subjective thoughts, communications, decisions and actions.

Instead of choosing one side, memes are:
- **External**: observable in behavior, artifacts, communication
- **Internal**: templates in cognitive structures
- **Both**: the interaction between them is what matters

## Aunger's Warning

Aunger pushed back harder. His core argument:

> *"All these suggestions bring Memetics back to **methodological reductionism**, where it does not belong."*

The proposals to "define units" and "measure effects" risk reducing Memetics to a set of techniques — missing the point.

### Methodological Reductionism vs Epistemological Holism

Aunger argued these should be unified:

| Methodological Reductionism | Epistemological Holism |
|---|---|
| Define specific units to study | See memes as part of complex systems |
| Measure individual effects | Understand systemic interaction |
| Study one meme at a time | Understand how memes shape wholes |

**To analyze**, you must define units (reduction).
**To understand**, you must see systemic interaction (holism).
The axiomatic presupposition: **memes influence the complex wholes they become part of.**

That's a **holistic and systemic perspective** — but you can only get there by defining your units first.

## The Evidence

Despite the pessimism from some pioneers, the numbers tell a different story:

**According to Google Scholar (1979–2013):**
- **6,538** academic articles mention "Memetics"
- **6,240** were written **after 2000**
- Since 2005: **4,957** articles focus on Memetics as an academic discipline

The field didn't die. It evolved.

## The Open Future

For Memetics to strengthen, it must:
- Stay open to perspectives from other research programmes
- Enrich itself with ideas from anthropology, neuroscience, cognitive science, communication studies
- Continue to evolve despite criticism

The criticisms weren't the end — they were growing pains.
